# First sales playbook

The goal right now isn't $10 a day. It's **the first 3 sales**, and learning who pays and why.
Plan on 30 minutes a day for two weeks.

## Who to message (most likely to pay first)

The data says which companies are hiring for a technology right now. That's most useful to people
who **sell services to those companies**:

1. **Dev agencies and consultancies:** a company hiring 5 Kubernetes engineers may hire contractors
   meanwhile.
2. **Freelance developers:** "who is hiring Rust people right now" is where their next client is.
3. **B2B salespeople selling developer tools:** hiring for a stack is a buying signal.
4. **Technical recruiters / staffing agencies:** useful to them, but the data has no contact
   details, so lead with the company list and the hiring volume.

Where they are: LinkedIn (search "agency owner" + your technology), r/freelance and
r/webdev (read each subreddit's rules first), Indie Hackers, the monthly Hacker News "Who is hiring"
and "Freelancer? Seeking freelancer?" threads, and local tech Slack/Discord groups.

## Rules

* Send each message yourself, by hand, to one person at a time, and personalise the first line.
  No bulk sending, no buying lists, no automation: that's spam, and it gets accounts banned.
* Lead with something free and useful (the 5-row preview or 3 company names), not the price.
* Never claim more than the data shows. Every number comes from the product's own page.
* If someone says no or doesn't answer twice, stop.

## Before you start

1. Finish setup so a buyer can actually pay: `automonetize go-live`, then
   `automonetize connect-marketing`.
2. In the control panel's Products tab, pick **one** product per message: the one that fits the
   person. Copy its link and three numbers from its card (postings, companies, top companies).

## 10 messages to adapt

Fill in the `[brackets]` from the product's card. Keep them short.

**1. Dev agency owner (LinkedIn)**
> Hi [name], I saw [agency] does a lot of [tech] work. I track public job postings: right now
> [N] companies are hiring [tech] engineers, e.g. [company 1], [company 2], [company 3]. Teams hiring
> that hard often take contractors while they recruit. Want the free 5-company preview?

**2. Freelance developer (LinkedIn / community DM)**
> Hey [name]: fellow [tech] person here. I put together a list of the [N] companies posting [tech]
> roles in the last few weeks ([X]% remote). Thought it might help with finding clients. Happy to
> send the free preview: [link]

**3. Dev-tool salesperson**
> Hi [name], hiring for a stack is a decent buying signal for [their product category]. This week
> [N] companies posted [tech] roles; [company 1] alone has [n] open. I sell the full list as a CSV
> ([price]). Free preview here if useful: [link]

**4. Technical recruiter**
> Hi [name], quick one: [N] companies are hiring [tech] engineers right now (from public postings,
> each with a link to the original). No contact details, just who's hiring, for what, and how many
> roles. Free preview: [link]. Would a weekly version be useful to you?

**5. Hacker News "Freelancer? Seeking freelancer?" thread (a comment, not a DM)**
> SEEKING WORK isn't me, but if you freelance in [tech]: I compiled the [N] companies currently
> posting [tech] roles ([X]% remote), with links to each posting. Free 5-row preview: [link]

**6. r/freelance-style subreddit (only where self-promotion is allowed)**
> I analysed [N] public [tech] job postings from the last month. Most active hirers: [company 1]
> ([n] roles), [company 2] ([n]), [company 3] ([n]); [X]% remote. Happy to answer questions about
> the data. Full list here if anyone wants it: [link]

**7. Indie Hackers post**
> I'm testing whether "who's hiring for technology X right now" is worth paying for. Example:
> [N] companies hiring [tech] this month. Free preview: [link]. If you'd pay for this, what format
> or technology would you want?

**8. Follow-up after someone looked at the free preview**
> Thanks for checking out the preview! The full [tech] list has [N] companies and [rows] postings,
> updated weekly. It's [price] here: [link]. Any technology or region you'd want instead?

**9. Asking for feedback (to someone who said no)**
> Totally fair, thanks for looking. One question, if you don't mind: what would have made it worth
> [price] to you: a different technology, contact details, a different format?

**10. Someone who bought**
> Thank you for buying! Was it what you expected? If you tell me what you used it for, I'll make
> the next version better for that. (And if anything's wrong, reply and I'll fix or refund it.)

## Track it

Keep a simple note: who you messaged, which product, the answer. After 30 messages you'll know
which buyer type and which technology answer. Tell me, and I'll point the product factory at
exactly that.
